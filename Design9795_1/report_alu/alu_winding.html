Aluminium winding for Design9795: cost against range

Recommended: OD 167 mm, 66 mm stack, 1 mm thinner back-iron with a 4.5 mm deeper slot, 10 turns of 4 × 1.70 mm aluminium. It saves ₹1314 per motor (30 % of the active-material cost), loses 1.09 % of range on the telematics cycle (1.21 % on WMTC Class 1), keeps the copper motor's torque within 1 % at every speed, and is 0.39 kg lighter. Aluminium cannot reach copper's efficiency within OD 167: the smallest range loss found is 0.74 % (OD 167, 78 mm stack, 8 turns), and it saves ₹747. Going from that design to the recommended one buys a further ₹567 for 0.35 % of range.

1. The trade-off

Points to the right have less range loss, points higher up save more. Every curve slopes down: moving to aluminium always costs some range, and saving more money costs more range. A larger OD moves the whole curve up and to the right, because the deeper slot holds more aluminium, so OD 167 gives the best designs. Range parity with copper is not reached anywhere within OD 167: aluminium needs 1.64× the copper's conductor area for the same resistance, while the largest slot that keeps the teeth and yoke from saturating gives at most 1.37–1.52×.

What the OD buys, at full performance:

  • OD 160 (same housing and lamination blank): the slot can only grow by thinning the yoke and teeth, which costs torque, so a longer stack (74–78 mm) is needed. Best range -1.25 %, best saving ₹1205 at -1.58 %.
  • OD 163: best range -0.96 %, best saving ₹1189 at -1.33 %.
  • OD 165: best range -0.98 %, best saving ₹1302 at -1.35 %.
  • OD 167: best range -0.74 %, best saving ₹1314 at -1.09 %. Each mm of OD adds 0.5 mm of slot depth and about 11 mm² of slot area per slot, for about 0.06 kg of stator steel (₹18).

At the original 64 mm stack, no aluminium design keeps full torque at every speed at any OD up to 167. With 10 turns, the higher resistance costs voltage and power above 2500 rpm; with fewer turns, the 360 A current limit costs low-speed torque. A slightly longer stack (66 mm at OD 167) restores the torque, so the stack length had to change. The rotor lamination and magnet cross-sections stay exactly as they are; only their length changes.

Motor-CAD splits its iron loss into stator and rotor, and into hysteresis and eddy over the whole machine (OP1: hysteresis 111 W, eddy 184 W; OP3: 86 W and 30 W), so its stator rows show the total only. At OP1, FEMM's copper iron loss is lower than Motor-CAD's (the residual hysteresis gap noted in the Lab validation) and its AC loss 4 % lower. Copper's efficiency is +0.46 points against Motor-CAD at OP1 and +0.08 at OP3.

  • OP1 (9000 rpm): all three aluminium designs are more efficient than copper (+0.97, +1.89 and +2.65 points). Copper's single 2.95 mm wire has 505 W of AC (eddy) winding loss at 750 Hz. Aluminium's thinner strands (1.50–1.70 mm) and higher resistivity cut this to 73–139 W, more than the extra DC loss. The 280 A DC limit applies, so the lower loss leaves more for the shaft.
  • OP2 (cruise): AC loss drops in the same way, and the recommended design is +1.39 points against copper. The longer stacks add iron loss and windage, so the least-range-loss design gains less here (+0.50).
  • OP3 (peak torque): this is where aluminium costs efficiency. DC winding loss is 1118 W against copper's 873 W in the recommended design (-1.77 points), 961 W in the cooler alternative (-0.49) and 852 W in the least-range-loss design (+0.38, at -0.6 % torque). The drop-in loses 4.6 points.

Over the telematics cycle (section 4 table), the AC winding loss saved by aluminium is smaller than the extra DC winding loss and iron loss, so the range change stays negative even though aluminium wins at OP1 and OP2.

Stator lamination
OD 160 → 167 mm. Slot depth 17.65 → 22.15 mm (slot bottom radius 71.15 → 75.65 mm). Back-iron 8.85 → 7.85 mm. Stack length
64 → 66 mm for the stator, rotor laminations and magnets (same cross-sections; magnets +2 mm long). Shaft, bearing span and housing length to suit (+2 mm, plus the longer end windings).
Winding
Copper SWG 11 (2.946 mm), 1 strand → aluminium EC-1350 grade 2 enamel, 4 strands in hand of 1.70 mm (IEC size, about 1.77 mm over enamel). 10 turns per coil, delta, 2 parallel paths, concentrated (one coil per tooth), same as copper. Insulated wire fill 0.510 (copper 0.513). Mean turn length 0.2 mm; resistance 17.25 mΩ per path at 100 °C (copper 13.48).
Terminations
Aluminium needs welded or crimped joints with aluminium-compatible lugs or Al-Cu bimetal transitions (no soldering), with sealing against galvanic corrosion at the Al-Cu interface. 4 strands make the coils easier to wind and to form round the tooth than one 2.95 mm wire.
Drive and calibration
Same 52 V / 360 A rms / 280 A DC limits. New flux-linkage and resistance tables in the controller (ψ and torque per amp change by about +3 % with the 66 mm stack). Resistance temperature coefficient 0.00403/K for aluminium.
Thermal
Winding loss at peak torque rises from 885 W to 1119 W (+26 %). The current density falls from 15.2 to 11.4 A/mm², the stator surface is larger and the winding is spread over a longer stack. Check the peak-torque duration in Motor-CAD Thermal before release. Mass
Winding 1.49 → 0.62 kg; stator steel 3.64 → 4.02 kg; rotor laminations + magnets 2.83 → 2.92 kg; active total 7.96 → 7.56 kg.

Thinning the yoke by 1 mm costs about 1.3 % of peak torque (yoke flux density 1.70 → 1.78 T); thinning the teeth by 1 mm costs about 1.9 %. A larger OD with the same yoke and teeth costs only 0.6 % at most (the slot gets deeper, so slot leakage rises slightly). That is why the best designs keep the teeth and thin the yoke by at most 1 mm.

9. Method and assumptions

Electromagnetics
Motor-CAD-equivalent FEMM Lab model of Design9795_1 (efficiency within 0.28 points of Motor-CAD over 840 map points), with the stator-variant corrections from FEMM. Turns: currents scaled by turns (same ampere-turns, same field), flux linkage × turns, end-winding inductance × turns². Stack length: flux, torque, iron loss and windage scale with length (2D); end-winding length is unchanged.
Winding
Aluminium EC-1350, 2.826 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m at 20 °C, 0.00403/K, 2703 kg/m³; winding at 100 °C. Insulated fill held at or below copper's 0.513 (grade-2 enamel build 0.042 + 0.014 d mm). Wire sizes: IEC 60317 and SWG from 1.0 mm, 1–6 strands in hand. Mean turn length from the stack length, tooth width and coil thickness. AC loss by the hybrid method (factor 2), slot field from FEMM.
Range
Telematics speed trace (Spoke V2, 10.7 km) and WMTC Class 1 (2 × part 1 reduced, 7.7 km). Vehicle GVW 757 kg, CdA 1.117 m², wheel radius 0.30 m, ratio 13.9; assumed Crr 0.015, gearbox 0.95, rotating mass factor 1.05, flat road, regenerative braking down to −26.4 N·m. Range change = energy per km at the motor terminals, copper ÷ aluminium − 1; inverter and battery taken as equal for all designs, so the battery-level change is about the same.
Cost
Active materials only, INR/kg: copper wire 1100, aluminium wire 370 (sensitivity 230–450), ferrite 800, M235-35A finished laminations 300 (net mass, stator + rotor), wire waste 5 %. Not included: lamination die, housing, terminations, winding process, scrap from the larger blank (the gross blank goes from 12.77 to 14.32 kg).
Performance criteria
Full: peak torque at 1000 rpm ≥ 99.5 % of copper's and envelope ≥ 99 % of copper's at every speed from 0 to 9500 rpm. Relaxed: envelope ≥ 97 %.
