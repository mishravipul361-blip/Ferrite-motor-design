Spoke V2: three operating points, one to one

Every quantity on your three Motor-CAD Operating Point screens is compared with the tuned FEMM model, in two ways. Same speed and torque: FEMM chooses its own MTPA / field-weakening current, which is what Motor-CAD's Operating Point does. Same current: Motor-CAD's phase current and phase advance are put straight into FEMM, which isolates the flux, torque and loss models from the choice of operating point.

4Deep analysis: what differs and why

A. Torque and flux linkage, the electromagnetic core: consistent

  • At identical currents, EM torque differs by −0.7 % (OP1), −2.0 % (OP2) and −1.1 % (OP3). Magnet torque differs by under 1 %. Reluctance torque differs by −3 to −11 %, but it is small (0.5–6 N·m), so the absolute difference is at most 0.24 N·m.
  • ψq agrees within 0.6–5 %, and the magnet flux linkage within 0.3–0.7 %. Ld agrees within 0.5–2 % and Lq within 2.4–7 % (OP2 has the largest gap: there the q-axis iron is least saturated, so the B-H curve at low field and the bridge geometry matter most).
  • ψd at OP1 (+10 % / −13 %) is a sensitivity effect, not a model error. In deep field weakening ψd = ψm + Ld·id = 20.5 − 17.5 ≈ 3 mVs is the small difference of two large terms, so 1 % on ψm or Ld becomes about 10 % on ψd. The voltage it produces is within 0.3 % (same operating point).
  • Peak torque −1.2 % (OP3). FEMM gives 44.3 N·m at 360 A where Motor-CAD gives 44.9 N·m at 361.7 A. At the same 361.7 A, FEMM gives 44.5 N·m (−1.0 %). The rest comes from 2D field differences in the saturated pole neck and the 6 × 6 d-q map, which is coarser than Motor-CAD's 30-point model. FEMM's MTPA angle is 23.2° against 20.8°: the torque-angle curve is flat near its peak, and the reluctance part, slightly lower in FEMM, shifts the optimum.

B. Operating-point selection: small current differences

  • At OP1 (maximum current at 9000 rpm), both tools hit the 280 A DC limit, but FEMM gets there with 135 A against 139.7 A phase current. FEMM's high-speed losses are slightly different, so it solves a slightly different point of equal torque (14.03 against 14.02 N·m). This changes the split between DC copper (−6 %) and AC copper and iron, but total loss agrees within 0.5 %.
  • At OP2 both use the full 49.4 V (0.3 % apart) and almost the same current (−1.8 %) and angle (−0.4°).

C. Iron loss: load dependence differs

  • OP3 (2000 rpm, peak torque): FEMM −16 % (91 W against 108 W). OP1 and OP2 (field weakening above 9000 rpm): FEMM +5 to +7 %.
  • FEMM's iron loss rises less with q-axis load and more with field weakening than Motor-CAD's. The likely reasons:
    1. Motor-CAD's exact iron-loss option (harmonics, minor loops with Kc 0.65) is not visible from the exports. FEMM uses peak-B in the stator and per-harmonic in the rotor, and on-load armature-reaction harmonics would add more loss with that option.
    2. FEMM's iron-loss map has 4 × 3 current points; peak torque lies at the edge of that grid, where the interpolation is least certain.
  • Effect on efficiency: −17 W at OP3 is +0.17 pt; +16 W at OP2 is −0.4 pt.

D. AC copper loss: frequency trend differs

  • FEMM −8 % at 2000 rpm (OP3), +0.4 % at 9000 rpm maximum current (OP1), +11 to +15 % at 9339 rpm light load (OP2).
  • FEMM uses the low-frequency round-wire formula with the average slot field (B̂² over the whole coil area) × 2. Motor-CAD's hybrid method uses the field at each conductor position and includes the skin-depth limit. At 778 Hz the wire is about 1.2 skin depths across, so the low-frequency formula starts to overstate, which fits OP2 being high. At low speed, Motor-CAD's position-resolved field near the slot opening is stronger than FEMM's average, which fits OP3 being low.

E. Torque ripple: matches at high speed, higher in FEMM at peak torque

  • Direct FEMM solves at Motor-CAD's currents give these ripples: OP1 2.47 N·m against 2.56 N·m (−4 %); OP2 3.49 N·m against 3.04 N·m (+15 %); OP3 2.36 N·m against 1.91 N·m (+24 %).
  • The direct solves also give the mean EM torque without d-q map interpolation: OP1 14.41 N·m against 14.51 N·m (−0.7 %), OP2 3.66 N·m against 3.65 N·m (+0.1 %), OP3 45.16 N·m against 45.52 N·m (−0.8 %). Torque agrees within 1 %.
  • The ripple pattern follows cogging. FEMM's cogging is about 30 % above Motor-CAD's (0.86 against 0.66 N·m) and mesh-converged in FEMM. Cogging is a large share of the ripple at OP3 (low speed, strongly saturated teeth) and at OP2 (small mean torque), and a small share at OP1. The most likely cause is the same as for cogging: Motor-CAD's coarser sampling of torque against rotor position (points per cycle), which rounds off peaks. FEMM uses 0.5° mech steps, which is 144 points per electrical cycle.

F. Exactly equal by construction

DC copper (R = 6.673 mΩ per phase at 100 °C), windage, friction (bearings) and magnet loss (0, ferrite) use Motor-CAD's own inputs, so they agree exactly at the same current and speed.
