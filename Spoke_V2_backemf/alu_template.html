2What changes when the conductor is aluminium

__MAT_ROWS__
PropertyCopperAluminium EC-1350Consequence

Torque comes from coil ampere-turns, so the magnetic design does not care what the coil is made of. With the same turns and the same current, aluminium gives exactly the same torque, back-EMF and torque-speed envelope. Only the winding losses change:

  • DC loss ×1.66. Resistance per turn is 1.66 times higher at 100 °C in the same cross-section. Matching copper resistance needs 66 % more conductor area. The copper slot is already at Motor-CAD wire fill 0.5175, so that area must come from a bigger slot or a longer stack.
  • AC (eddy) loss falls. Strand eddy loss scales with conductivity × d⁴. Aluminium carries only 61 % of copper's eddy loss in the same wire, and splitting each turn into 2–4 thinner strands cuts it much further. In this motor, AC loss is the largest winding loss above about 7000 rpm (501 W of 1353 W at OP1), so this is a real gain.
  • Field weakening costs more. Above base speed the drive pushes a demagnetising d-axis current that produces no torque. The ampere-turns this needs are set by magnet flux and inductance, and they do not fall with a longer stack. More aluminium resistance therefore shows up fully at high speed and light load (OP2).

The scaling rule used for every design below: with stack factor k = L/63 and turns factor s = Nph/20, the 2D field is a function of ampere-turns only.

4Design levers

4.1 Turns and stack length keeps torque and voltage

A longer stack gives more flux per turn. Fewer turns then bring the back-EMF back to the copper value, and each turn gets a bigger share of the slot: per-turn area grows as 1/s. For the same torque the ampere-turns fall roughly as 1/k, so DC loss falls by about k² while the mean turn grows only by 2·ΔL. The winding stays delta with 2 parallel paths. 4 paths are not allowed: adjacent 12s/10p coils are 30° el apart, so paralleling them would drive circulating current. Turns per phase therefore come in steps of 2: 20 → 18 (70 mm), 16 (78.75 mm), 14 (90 mm). Each turn count was swept from 63 to 99 mm in 3 mm steps; lengths away from 20·63/Nph change the envelope and are filtered by the envelope check.

The cost is iron. Stator and rotor iron loss, magnet mass and lamination mass all grow in proportion to k. At high speed and light load iron loss dominates, so a longer stack lowers OP2 efficiency even while it raises OP3.

4.2 Slot area new lamination die

The extra slot area has to come from the yoke (deeper slot) or the teeth (narrower tooth), because the OD and bore stay fixed. A ferrite machine runs its iron at low flux density at no load. At peak current, though, the thinner yoke saturates and peak torque falls; the deeper slots lose noticeably more (table). The design model applies these FEMM torque, flux and iron-loss changes, and the envelope check rejects any design that cannot hold the copper torque-speed curve. Five stators were re-solved in FEMM, each on a 4 × 4 (id, iq) grid at two rotor positions (160 solves). OD 160 mm, bore 107 mm, slot opening and tip unchanged.

Each design uses the largest standard round wire (IEC 60317 metric, SWG or AWG, grade-2 enamel) that keeps the insulated-wire slot fill at or below the copper design's 0.5175, for 1 to 4 strands in hand. 4.4 Lamination thickness material change

Iron loss in this motor is mostly eddy-current loss at high speed (OP2: 164 W eddy, 96 W hysteresis out of 260 W). Classical eddy loss scales with thickness². Going from M235-35A to a 0.27 mm or 0.20 mm grade pays for the extra iron of a longer stack. It helps the copper design too, by +0.6 and +0.7 points on the map mean, so it is a separate improvement. It is included here because it is the only lever that recovers OP2 efficiency once the stack grows.

Thin-gauge loss modelled as eddy × (t/0.35)², hysteresis ×1.0 (0.27 mm) and ×1.1 (0.20 mm), keeping the Motor-CAD Steinmetz fit and the 1.6 build factor. Confirm with the supplier's loss curves before committing.

8Manufacturing and design changes beyond the electromagnetics

  • Terminations. Aluminium cannot be soldered. Use crimp or ultrasonic welding to tinned-copper lugs, or TIG/ultrasonic Al–Cu transition joints at the star/delta bus. Bolted Al joints need Belleville washers and contact grease against creep and fretting.
  • Winding. Aluminium magnet wire is softer and has about a third of copper's tensile strength. Reduce winding tension, use larger bend radii on the tooth corners and check enamel crack resistance after forming. 3–4 strands in hand need a tensioner per strand and a parallel-wire guide.
  • Insulation and impregnation. Higher thermal expansion (23 vs 17 µm/m·K) gives more relative movement against enamel and varnish. Use a resin system rated for it and run thermal-cycling tests.
  • Thermal. The recommended design has equal or lower total loss at the operating points and a longer stack with more cooling surface, so winding temperature should stay at or below the copper design. Re-run the Motor-CAD thermal model with the aluminium winding properties and the new stack.
  • Mechanical. A longer stack lengthens the rotor (bearing span, first bending mode at 12 000 rpm), the housing, and the magnet and lamination count. Check the rotor critical speed and the shaft.
  • Inverter. Same 52 V, 360 A rms and 280 A DC; back-EMF and inductances are kept close to the copper design, so the controller calibration (MTPA tables) only needs regenerating, not redesigning.

9Assumptions and next steps

  • 2D FEA scaling: end-region fringing is ignored, so the longer stacks are slightly optimistic, by about 1 % in torque. End-winding inductance is scaled with turns², and the end-winding length per turn is taken as unchanged (75.6 mm, −2 mm per mm of narrower tooth).
  • Winding and magnets at 100 °C in all designs (same as the Motor-CAD Lab run). Aluminium resistivity 2.826 µΩ·cm at 20 °C, α = 0.00403 /K.
  • AC loss uses the same Motor-CAD hybrid adjustment factor (2) for all strand counts. Multi-strand coils can also carry circulating current between strands if they are not transposed; Motor-CAD's full hybrid AC model should confirm the chosen strand count.
  • Slot variants were solved on a 4 × 4 current grid, used as corrections on the 6 × 6 Lab map. Rotor iron loss is scaled with stack length only (slot opening unchanged).
  • Thin-lamination losses are first-order estimates (section 4.4).
