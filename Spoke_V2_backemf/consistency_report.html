2Why the earlier FEMM results were not consistent

Torque, flux linkage, current, voltage, DC copper loss and mechanical loss already matched Motor-CAD (within about 2 %; DC copper and mechanical exactly). Every missing watt was iron loss: FEMM's iron loss was 56 % lower than Motor-CAD's, so the FEMM efficiency map came out about 2 points too high, especially at high speed. There were four causes.

cause 1Eddy-current term missing the 2π² factor

Motor-CAD's Steinmetz eddy term is 2π² · Keddy · f² · B². FEMM used Keddy · f² · B², which made eddy loss about 20× too small. Eddy loss came out 97 % low, and that is the part that grows with speed².

Evidence: your M235-35A loss chart. At 2500 Hz and 0.5 T, Kh·f·B^2.07 + 2π²·Keddy·f²·B² = 15 + 68 = 83 W/kg, which matches the plotted ~85 W/kg. Without 2π² it would be 18.5 W/kg. At Motor-CAD's no-load point (1000 rpm), FEMM eddy with 2π² is 2.4 W against Motor-CAD's 2.43 W.

cause 2Iron loss from a single snapshot at the fundamental only

v1 estimated iron loss from the flux density at four rotor positions, assumed a sinusoidal field at the electrical frequency, and approximated rotor loss from slot ripple. That misses how iron loss grows with load and field weakening, and nearly all of the rotor iron loss. The rotor sees no fundamental, only slot and stator-MMF harmonics, so v1's rotor loss was 93 % low (about 5 W against 44 W at 4000 rpm).

Motor-CAD instead runs a full electrical cycle (30 points, "FEA Map 30 points" in its model build) at each loss-map point and evaluates the loss element by element.

cause 3 (found while fixing 2)FEMM remeshes at every rotor position

Rotating the rotor with mi_moverotate makes FEMM rebuild the whole mesh at each position; only about 30 % of element centroids stay in place. On first-order elements, flux density jumps from element to element, and those jumps appear as broadband "harmonics", which the eddy term (∝ n²) amplifies. The first full-cycle attempt overstated stator loss about 2× and rotor loss 10–25×.

Fix: FEMM's air-gap element (AGE). Stator and rotor meshes stay fixed, and the rotor angle is the AGE boundary's inner angle. Check: flux linkages match the moving-rotor model to 4 digits (0.00506 against 0.00505 Wb at 2.4°). Rotor no-load loss became 0.8 W against Motor-CAD's 1.2 W.

cause 4Which Steinmetz formulation matches Motor-CAD

With clean full-cycle fields, three formulations were tested offline against every Motor-CAD map point, at Motor-CAD's own currents and speeds:

Applying every harmonic to the stator overstates it (+94 %): the stator yoke's 9th/11th harmonics add large eddy loss that Motor-CAD's results do not contain. Peak-B Steinmetz in the stator, which is what alternating stator flux calls for, matches within +11 %. The rotor has no fundamental, so it needs the harmonic spectrum (−12 %). Chosen model: stator peak-B per element and component; rotor Steinmetz per harmonic. Total iron loss comes out +7 % on average and within ±14 % at every speed.

Remaining differences, and where they come from
  • Stator iron loss +11 to +16 % (mean |error| 15 %): FEMM's no-load stator loss is somewhat higher than Motor-CAD's. Likely sources are the extra-fine 2D field resolution near the tooth tips and the 30-point cycle. This is not tuned with a fudge factor.
  • Rotor iron loss −12 %: rotor harmonics are evaluated over one electrical cycle (as in Motor-CAD), and FEMM's mesh is coarser in the rotor bridges.
  • AC copper loss ±9 %: the hybrid round-wire estimate uses the FEMM slot field with Motor-CAD's ×2 adjustment. No bias, scatter only.
  • d-axis current and phase-advance relative error ~19 %: these are small numbers near zero at low torque. In absolute terms the difference is under 1 A and about 0.1°.
