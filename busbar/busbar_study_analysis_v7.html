    Method and assumptions

    Electrical model. Every conductor (ring segments, tabs, links or jumpers, coils, terminals, welds and bolts) is a resistance R = ρ·L/A with copper ρ = 1.72e-5 Ω·mm at 20 °C (α = 0.00393 /K). The network is solved by nodal analysis at two instants of balanced three-phase line currents (360 A rms line, 208 A phase); because the network is linear, each branch current is A·sin θ + B·cos θ and its rms is √((A² + B²)/2). Line-to-line resistance comes from a 1 A injection between two terminals.

    Coil. SWG 11 (2.946 mm bare), the delivered v7 coil path (1962.96 mm, of which 1892.16 mm winding), its lead tips (F at r 67.43, S at r 74.0) and end-turn height (z 44.84).

    Score. Each term is normalised to 1 at the v7 value or at the rule limit: busbar loss / v7, height / v7, copper / v7, path imbalance / limit, terminal span / limit, distance of the cable exit from the v7 exit / 180°. A goal is a set of weights; the explorers let you set your own.

    Rules. Bend strain ≤ 25 % (DIN 6935: strip t/(2r+t), wire r/R); insulation by IEC 60034-1 test 2U + 1000 V at ¼ of dielectric strength with a moulding minimum wall; creepage IEC 60664-1 (PD2, group I); stamping web and slot ≥ sheet thickness (ASTM B152 practice); copper inside the stator OD by the margin; current density, imbalance and terminal span and pitch limits. Coil-bend results come from the CAD coil and are reported separately (they need a coil change, not a busbar change).

    Limits of this study. DC resistance only (no skin or proximity effect, no inductance; at speed the coil inductance evens out the path currents, so the imbalance figures are a stall-case worst). Joint resistances are assumptions (10 µΩ weld, 20 µΩ bolt). DC-link voltage is assumed 96 V unless a condition row says otherwise. Single-plane coils are the real v7 path; its jumper route and lead jog are proposals, not CAD.
